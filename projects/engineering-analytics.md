# Engineering Analytics and Build-versus-Buy Evaluation

## Context

An enterprise modernization organization was evaluating how to aggregate engineering-delivery and operational metadata across systems such as GitLab, GitHub, Azure DevOps, Jira, ServiceNow, and Confluence.

## Matthew's contribution

Matthew served as technical lead and primary architectural driver.

He:

- Established architecture, ingestion, normalization, extensibility, reporting, and backend direction.
- Developed an Express.js backend API.
- Identified scalability, maintainability, firewall, ownership, and extensibility risks in heavily customized low-code approaches and redirected the initiative toward a more maintainable architecture.
- Led build-versus-buy analysis comparing internal development with commercial engineering-intelligence platforms.
- Directly evaluated Jellyfish, DX, and Oobeya; another contributor researched the remaining vendor set.
- Evaluated DevEx, DORA, developer productivity, operational health, implementation effort, data freshness, cost, latency, integration depth, adoption, security, SaaS retention, outbound telemetry, AI usage, and long-term operational ownership.
- Worked with security and compliance stakeholders to bound ingestion around operational metadata rather than source code or sensitive descriptive content.
- Produced executive-level comparisons, identified DX and Hivel.ai as front-runners within the broader evaluation, and recommended phased adoption approaches.
- Mentored and coordinated supporting contributors.

## Technical environment

Express.js, JavaScript/TypeScript, GitLab, GitHub, Azure DevOps, Jira, ServiceNow, Confluence, engineering telemetry, operational metadata, analytics pipelines, DevEx concepts, DORA-oriented measurement, commercial engineering-intelligence platforms, and federal security/data-boundary considerations.

## Evidence boundaries

- Matthew led architecture and evaluation; the initiative did not become a complete mature production analytics platform during the approved scope.
- Vendor evaluation is not implementation ownership of the products evaluated.
- Funding reductions stopped procurement. No vendor was purchased, piloted, or implemented.
- Jellyfish explicitly declined the federal/FedRAMP adaptation effort.
- Vendor names, front-runner status, and recommendation context describe the evaluation outcome, not a final procurement or production decision.
